Back to the opening question. Suggested answer: a developer is someone who solves the right problems and takes responsibility for the result. AI is a powerful tool in that job. Then open the floor (about 15 minutes). Likely questions and short answers: “Will AI replace developers?” The work is changing: less typing, more designing, reviewing, and deciding. People who use AI well and can judge its output are in a strong position. Entry-level tasks are changing too, which is why fundamentals and a portfolio matter more. “Is using AI for assignments cheating?” Follow your lecturer’s rules. Use AI as a tutor, be open about how you used it, and never submit code you can’t explain. “Which AI tool should I learn?” Any mainstream assistant is fine to start. The skills transfer: clear context, verification, and testing. “Do I still need algorithms and data structures?” Yes. They’re how you catch AI’s mistakes, and they still come up in technical interviews. “How do I get my first internship?” One or two real projects with documented decisions, an active GitHub, joining communities, and applying early. Close by thanking the audience and sharing your contact details.
